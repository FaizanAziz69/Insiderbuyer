Key points

  • Gold near US$4,650/oz sits above the US$4,500 upside case in White Gold's PEA, built on a US$3,600 base.
  • WGO is up roughly 340% over the past year, against about 62% for the VanEck Junior Gold Miners ETF (GDXJ) and 34% for gold.
  • Agnico Eagle's 19% sits one point below the 20% Canadian take-over-bid threshold — positioned, not committed.

Gold near US$4,650 an ounce lifts every explorer in the Yukon. It does not explain a 340% year when the VanEck Junior Gold Miners ETF (GDXJ), the sector's standard benchmark, returned about 62%. The metal delivered a third of the move, junior-miner beta roughly doubled it, and the rest is company-specific — though White Gold is not even the leader of its own district.

On August 10 White Gold published its maiden PEA: a C$1,911 million after-tax NPV at a 5% discount rate, a 38% IRR and a 1.7-year payback, on a 9.4-year open pit averaging 188,000 ounces a year. The assumption underneath matters more — the base case takes US$3,600 gold and the upside case US$4,500, so spot already trades above the optimistic scenario in a study published two and a half weeks ago.

Who Are The Insiders Behind White Gold Corp?

Agnico Eagle owns 19% by White Gold's own disclosure. Kinross Gold, which sold the properties in at the 2016 formation, holds roughly 15%. Larger than either, Eric Sprott is reported to hold about 46% fully diluted. Published producer figures range 15%–19.9% by source and date.

David D'Onofrio is Chief Executive Officer and also CFO of PowerOne Capital Markets, the merchant bank he joined in 2009 which has backed White Gold since inception. The geology sits with co-founder Shawn Ryan, the prospector the district is named for, and VP Exploration Dylan Langille, formerly of Great Bear.

Which is where the 19% matters. Canadian take-over bid rules trigger at 20% of voting securities. Agnico sits one point below that line — about as much as it can hold without being required to bid for the whole company. Positioned rather than committed, with every milestone raising the cost of staying that way.

Are Insiders Buying or Selling?

Buying — and only buying. White Gold has no US listing, so its insiders report to Canada's SEDI system rather than the SEC. According to SEDI filings reviewed by InsiderBuying.com, insiders made 12 open-market purchases in the twelve months to August 27 and zero sales.

Eleven of the twelve were the chief executive's. Between August 22 and September 17, 2025, David D'Onofrio bought 882,500 shares on the open market for C$518,562, at prices from C$0.475 to C$0.76 — an average of C$0.5876. At C$2.42 that position has quadrupled. His holding went from 3.73 million to 4.73 million shares.

The timing is the point. Every one of those purchases came eleven months before the August 2026 PEA and the 340% run that followed. The chief executive was buying at under C$0.80 when the market was not.

The rest of the insider record is a single event. On October 15, 2025 the company closed a private placement at C$0.85 with a half-warrant at C$1.15, and the register bought into it: Agnico Eagle took 2,741,538 shares for C$2.33 million, PowerOne Capital 500,000 through Pasquale DiCapo, and D'Onofrio and Langille added 116,500 and 23,400 respectively. PowerOne's backing is a filing, not a characterisation.

What the Capex Number Actually Means

The C$1,050 million initial capital is not a bill White Gold intends to pay. Junior developers rarely build or operate mines: they find deposits, prove economic viability and sell them. The business is de-risking a property until it is attractive to a company that does build mines.

So the PEA is not a construction plan. It is the milestone that moves White Gold from explorer toward advanced-stage developer — where it stops only finding minerals and starts spending on the engineering that makes an asset shovel-ready, permitted and priced.

Two caveats. A PEA is the earliest economic study and includes inferred material with no demonstrated viability. And being positioned for a bid is not receiving one — a 340% re-rating off a C$0.53 low reverses quickly if a milestone slips.

What to Watch

The spin-out is the near-term catalyst. Shareholders approved moving six Yukon properties into W2 Critical Minerals Corp on August 11, with court and TSXV approval outstanding. W2 shares go out one per five WGO shares held immediately prior to the effective date — not the June 29 record date, which governed only voting — and that date is unannounced.
