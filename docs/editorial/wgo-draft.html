Gold near US$4,650 an ounce lifts every explorer in the Yukon. It does not explain a 340% year when the VanEck Junior Gold Miners ETF (GDXJ), the sector's standard benchmark, returned about 62%. The metal delivered a third of the move, junior-miner beta roughly doubled it, and the rest is company-specific — though White Gold is not even the leader of its own district.

On August 10 White Gold published its maiden PEA: a C$1,911 million after-tax NPV at a 5% discount rate, a 38% IRR and a 1.7-year payback, on a 9.4-year open pit averaging 188,000 ounces a year. The assumption underneath matters more — the base case takes US$3,600 gold and the upside case US$4,500, so spot already trades above the optimistic scenario in a study published two and a half weeks ago.

North of 60 Mining News called it a robust Yukon mine; consensus is a Strong Buy at C$5.18.

What the capex number actually means

The C$1,050 million initial capital is not a bill White Gold intends to pay. Junior developers rarely build or operate mines: they find deposits, prove economic viability and sell them. The business is de-risking a property until it is attractive to a company that does build mines.

So the PEA is not a construction plan. It is the milestone that moves White Gold from explorer toward advanced-stage developer — where it stops only finding minerals and starts spending on the engineering that makes an asset shovel-ready, permitted and priced.

Which is where the 19% matters. Canadian take-over bid rules are triggered at 20% of a company's voting securities. Agnico Eagle sits one percentage point below that line — about as much equity as it can hold without being required to bid formally for the whole company. It is positioned rather than committed, and every milestone from here raises the cost of staying that way.

Two caveats. A PEA is the earliest economic study and includes inferred material with no demonstrated viability. And being positioned for a bid is not receiving one — a 340% re-rating off a C$0.53 low reverses quickly if a milestone slips.

The spin-out is the near-term catalyst. Shareholders approved moving six Yukon properties into W2 Critical Minerals Corp on August 11, with court and TSXV approval outstanding. W2 shares go out one per five WGO shares held immediately prior to the effective date — not the June 29 record date, which governed only voting — and that date is unannounced.
