Key points

  • Gold is trading near US$4,650/oz — above the US$4,500 upside case in White Gold's own maiden PEA, which was built on a US$3,600 base assumption.
  • White Gold Corp (TSXV: WGO) is up roughly 340% over the past year, against +72% for the junior gold miners index and +34% for gold itself. The metal explains part of the move, not most of it.
  • The register is the story: Agnico Eagle owns 19% by the company's own disclosure, Kinross Gold roughly 15%, and Eric Sprott is reported to hold about 46% fully diluted.

Gold near US$4,650 an ounce lifts every explorer in the Yukon. It does not explain why one of them is up more than four times the junior gold index over the past year. That gap is what this article is about.

White Gold Corp (TSXV: WGO) closed at C$2.42 on August 27, a market capitalisation of about C$521 million on 221.5 million shares, against a 52-week range of C$0.53 to C$2.47. The one-year return is roughly 340%. For comparison over the same period: gold itself is up about 34%, the VanEck Junior Gold Miners ETF about 72%, and among named Yukon peers, Banyan Gold is up about 228%, Snowline Gold about 96% and Sitka Gold about 40%.

So the brief version is this: the metal delivered roughly a third of a move, junior-miner beta roughly doubled that, and White Gold delivered five times the index. Something company-specific happened.

What happened: the PEA

On August 10 the company published its maiden Preliminary Economic Assessment on the White Gold Project. The base case: an after-tax net present value of C$1,911 million at a 5% discount rate, a 38% internal rate of return, and a 1.7-year payback. That is a 9.4-year, 12,000-tonne-per-day open pit averaging 188,000 ounces of gold a year, and 223,000 ounces a year across the first five years. Initial capital is estimated at C$1,050 million including contingencies, with an all-in sustaining cost of US$1,480 an ounce.

The number that matters most is the gold price underneath it. The base case assumes US$3,600 an ounce, and the company also modelled an upside case at US$4,500, which produced a C$3 billion NPV and a 52% IRR.

Gold is currently trading above that upside case. A study published two and a half weeks ago is already conservative against spot.

The resource behind it, from the August 2025 estimate: 1,732,300 indicated ounces at 1.53 g/t gold, plus 1,265,900 inferred ounces at 1.22 g/t — about three million ounces in total, across a land package of 15,364 quartz claims over 21 properties and 305,102 hectares, which the company describes as roughly 40% of the district.

Who is behind it

The share register answers the headline better than the market does. Agnico Eagle Mines owns 19% of the company by White Gold's own corporate disclosure. Kinross Gold — which sold the White Gold properties into the company at its 2016 formation — holds roughly 15%. Larger than either, Eric Sprott is reported to hold approximately 46% on a fully diluted basis through personal holdings, having led or participated in a series of the company's placements.

Published figures for the two producers vary between 15% and 19.9% depending on the source and the date, because every flow-through placement moves them; treat them as approximate and the company's own 19% for Agnico as the anchor.

David D'Onofrio is Chief Executive Officer and a director. He is also the Chief Financial Officer of PowerOne Capital Markets, the Toronto merchant bank he joined in 2009 — a Chartered Professional Accountant and Schulich graduate whose background is corporate finance in natural resources rather than geology. PowerOne has been involved with White Gold since inception, and a firm of that kind seconding its own CFO into the chief executive's chair is a statement about how it reads the balance sheet it helped assemble.

The geology sits with two people who are genuinely hard to replicate. Shawn Ryan — the prospector whose Yukon staking work is the reason the district carries the name — is co-founder, Chief Technical Advisor and a director. Dylan Langille is VP Exploration; he was on the core discovery team at the Great Bear project in Red Lake, Ontario, and stayed to lead exploration there after Kinross acquired it. Hans Smit, a geologist with four decades in the field, joined as a technical advisor in May.

The spin-out, and the part worth reading carefully

White Gold is moving six critical-mineral properties in west-central Yukon into a subsidiary, W2 Critical Minerals Corp. The logic is that copper, molybdenum and tungsten ground carries close to no value inside a gold explorer's rating, and some value on its own. White Gold retains roughly 19% of the new company.

The six are not blank ground. Bridget is an untested 3 by 3.5 km porphyry system with rock samples to 3,650 ppm molybdenum and 1,854 ppm copper. Toonie's Deux target returned 24.4 m of 0.235 g/t gold and 525.4 ppm copper from surface in a 2023 RAB hole.

Wolf's Taurus target returned 6.55 g/t gold over 1.52 m, Hayes sits 38 km east of Casino with eight chargeability anomalies identified, and Loonie and Hunker carry copper-silver values with IP support. They are early-stage targets, not resources.

Shareholders approved the reorganisation on August 11, with 46.12% of outstanding shares voted, and the court hearing for the final order was set for August 13. The transaction remains subject to final court and TSX Venture Exchange approval.

The distribution mechanism matters more than the vote. W2 shares are to be issued as a dividend-in-kind, one W2 share for every five WGO shares held immediately prior to the effective date — not as of the June 29 record date, which governed only who could vote at the meeting. On the company's disclosed terms the entitlement attaches at the effective date, and no effective date has been announced. Both the terms and the timing are the company's to change, so that belongs in the circular rather than in a paragraph like this one.

The district, and the risks

The Yukon's Tombstone belt now holds a cluster of multi-million-ounce systems — Snowline's Valley, Banyan's AurMac, Sitka's RC, alongside the past-producing Brewery Creek and Eagle Gold mines. The senior interest is not confined to White Gold: Newmont retains about 27% of Fuerte Metals, which holds the Coffee project, and Agnico Eagle sits on the register of both Fuerte and White Gold. Klondike Gold, Cascadia Minerals and Silver North Resources are also active in the territory.

Three things cut the other way, and they belong in the same article. Initial capital of C$1,050 million sits against a C$521 million market capitalisation, so the project as designed cannot be built without substantial dilution, a partner, or both — and the two producers on the register are the obvious candidates for the second option. A PEA is the earliest of the economic studies and includes inferred material that has no demonstrated economic viability. And a 340% move off a C$0.53 low is exactly the kind of re-rating that reverses quickly when a venture-listed explorer misses a catalyst.
