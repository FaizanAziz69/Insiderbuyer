What it is
The congressional counterpart to the Insider Quality Score: one 0–100 score per STOCK that measures how strong the congressional buying signal on that stock is right now. It powers a ranked index/table, the Congress bubbles visualizer, the gold-tier system, alerts, and the strategy library.
Relationship to prior briefs
CTS (Brief v5) scores one politician × one contract intersection — it becomes an input here. Brief v7 politician grades become the 'who is buying' quality input. CQS rolls everything up to the stock.
Working name
CQS — pairs with IQS so readers learn one system. George confirms.

1. Qualification — which stocks get scored
A stock enters the CQS universe only when at least one of the following fires inside a rolling 90-day window (on transaction date, using filings available at scoring time):
	•	Size trigger: any single member purchase in a disclosure band whose lower bound is ≥ $100,001 (i.e., the $100,001–$250,000 band and above). PTRs report ranges, not amounts, so '$100k+' is implemented as a band floor — exact and auditable.
	•	Cluster trigger: two or more distinct members (households) buying the same stock inside the window, any size ≥ $15,001.
	•	Influence trigger: any purchase ≥ $15,001 by a member whose committee has jurisdiction over an agency that awarded the company a material contract (the CTS condition from Brief v5).
Exclusions (noise filters): ETFs, mutual funds, Treasuries and municipal bonds; purchases the filing marks as made by a third-party manager or held in a blind/qualified trust (lower informational content — tracked, scored at 0.25x); dividend reinvestments; exchanges/rollovers. Sales are tracked and shown but contribute only lightly (§2, C8).

4. What I added beyond your spec — and why each strengthens the signal
Your three pillars — clustering, $100k+ positions, and committee-to-contract influence — are the core. I added seven things; each addresses a specific way congressional data produces false signals:
	•	Buyer track record (C5). Treats a cluster of historically accurate traders differently from a cluster of historically poor ones. This is the idea that makes IQS work, transplanted; without it, every politician counts the same.
	•	Relative conviction (C6). A $100k buy is routine for a member who always trades $250k blocks and exceptional for one whose median is $15k. Scoring against each member's own baseline catches genuine departures from habit. First-ever purchases score higher for the same reason.
	•	Bipartisan cluster bonus. Same-party clusters can reflect shared ideology or the same advisers. Members of opposing parties independently buying the same stock is harder to explain by politics and more likely to reflect information about the business.
	•	Freshness decay (C7). PTRs can arrive up to 45 days after the trade. A signal half-priced-in by the time it's public must score lower, or the index will be ranked by history rather than opportunity.
	•	Insider overlap multiplier. Connects CQS to the IQS system you already own — the one piece of this index no competitor can replicate.
	•	Legislative catalyst. Extends 'influence' from contracts to pending legislation in the members' committees — the second channel you named in the gold-tier brief.
	•	Noise exclusions and managed-account discount. Removes the funds, bonds and third-party-managed trades that inflate most congress trackers' activity counts without carrying member decision-making.
4.1 One caution that protects the whole index
Quiver's September 2026 insider-strategy test found that naive cluster buying badly underperformed on the S&P 500. Clusters are not automatically signal. That is why cluster breadth is only 20% of CQS, why track record and relative conviction check who is in the cluster, and why every weight above is provisional until the calibration in §5 proves it earns its place. If calibration shows a component adds nothing, it is cut — the index is better with seven honest components than eight hopeful ones.
5. Calibration & validation protocol
	•	Weights and thresholds are fitted on a training window only (disclosure history through Dec 2023), using point-in-time data: a trade enters the score on its FILING date, never its transaction date. Anything else is lookahead.
	•	Test: forward returns of CQS deciles at 1, 3, 6 and 12 months, measured from filing date, vs. sector-matched benchmarks. The score is valid only if higher deciles monotonically outperform lower deciles in the holdout (2024 → present), run once per Backtest Runbook v1.
	•	Component ablation: each component removed in turn; any whose removal doesn't degrade holdout decile spread is dropped or down-weighted.
	•	Publish the calibration summary on the methodology page (decile chart, labeled hypothetical). A score that openly shows its validation is a score allocators and journalists will cite.

	•	CQS Index page: the ranked table above, sortable on any column, filters for sector, market cap band, party mix, committee, overlap flag, grade, freshness.

8. Legal frame (unchanged, enforced in templates)
CQS measures the strength of a disclosed, lawful trading signal. It is never described as a measure of impropriety. Brief v5 rules apply verbatim: neutral verbs, banned-word list, estimates labeled, transaction vs. filing dates distinguished, Stage 5 verification and append-only corrections, counsel review before launch.
